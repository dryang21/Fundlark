# Funding through affiliations and research fields

Build a small, editable profile from available context or a supplied CV: research
topics; career stage; professional memberships and grades; current institution,
department and appointment; and location. Preserve the supporting CV line and any
dates. Use only information needed for this search; do not publish these details.

Distinguish membership evidence from a society mention in a publication, journal,
conference, award or collaborator biography. A paper in IEEE Transactions is not
proof of IEEE membership. Society Fellow status is not the same as a funded
research fellowship. Ask for membership confirmation when an opportunity needs it.

Separate current appointments from education, former positions, visiting roles and
collaborators' affiliations. A Stanford degree does not establish access to Stanford
internal grants. Do not merge similar institution names without checking identity.
Use current appointment evidence if available and let the user correct the profile.

Search three complementary routes:

- **Industry:** select sponsors by field. Include computing/AI programs, cloud or
  hardware credits, medical-device investigator-initiated research, pharmaceutical
  independent research, and engineering/materials collaborations as relevant.
  Cash, credits, equipment, sponsored contracts and product support differ. Check
  IP, PI/institution, geography and submission-window rules on the official page.
- **Societies:** look at IEEE and the relevant IEEE technical society/chapter for
  engineering/computing; ASTRO for radiation oncology; AAPM for medical physics;
  extend to other societies suggested by the user's actual field. Include seed
  grants, early-career awards and fellowships; label travel/meeting support separately.
  Society sources can be relevant without membership, but member eligibility is unknown.
- **Institutions:** search the current institution's official research office,
  internal seed/pilot grants, department, cancer center/CTSA, postdoc office and
  limited-submission competitions. For Stanford, start with its Seed Funding portal
  and relevant programs such as Bio-X. For unlisted institutions, search their
  official domain and verify identity instead of assuming coverage stops at Stanford.

A limited-submission opportunity is an institutional selection route, not necessarily
internal money. Record the institution's nomination deadline separately from the
funder's deadline. Check faculty-PI, trainee, department and appointment restrictions.
Some portals require institutional sign-in: state the gap and ask the user to supply
the announcement or check their portal; do not claim to have reviewed private calls.

Explain why each source was selected and which affiliation is relevant. Never infer
an open call, membership requirement or eligibility from a directory listing alone.
